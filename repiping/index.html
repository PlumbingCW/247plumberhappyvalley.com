Repiping in Happy Valley, OR

Pinhole leaks, rusty water and pressure that keeps dropping point to pipes at the end of their life. We repipe Happy Valley homes in PEX or copper, whole house or one section at a time.

Repiping means replacing the supply lines that carry water through the house. It sounds drastic, and for the right home it is the most cost-effective way to stop a cycle of leaks. A single pinhole leak is a repair. Three in two years is a pattern, and the pipes are telling you something. If water is escaping now, start with burst pipe repair and then plan the longer fix.

Our plumber begins by finding out why the pipes are failing. Age, pipe material, water chemistry and pressure all matter. A repipe that ignores the cause can fail the same way. That is why the assessment comes before the quote, and why you will get an honest answer if a partial repair is the smarter path.

Signs it may be time to repipe

Repeated leaks in different spots, brown or rust-colored water when a faucet is first opened, low pressure that has gotten worse over the years, visible corrosion or green stains at copper joints, and a house built with a pipe type known for failures all point toward repiping. A galvanized steel system, polybutylene lines or early plastic fittings with a poor track record are common triggers.

Questions we ask during an assessment

  • What type of pipe and fittings are in the home, and how old are they?
  • Where have leaks occurred, and are the repairs holding?
  • Is the water discolored, or is pressure lower than it used to be?
  • Where do the lines run: slab, crawlspace, attic or walls?

PEX or copper?

PEX is flexible plastic tubing that can run in long continuous lengths with fewer fittings, tolerates freezing better than rigid pipe and installs quickly. Copper is rigid, long-lived and trusted, but costs more in materials and labor. Both can be done properly under Oregon code, and the plumber explains which one suits your house, budget and preference.

In many Happy Valley homes, PEX with a manifold system is the common choice.

What a repipe involves

The plumber maps the existing lines, plans new routes, opens small access holes where needed, installs the new piping and connects every fixture. The water is off during parts of the day, and we plan around your household's needs.

After the new lines are pressure tested, the old pipe is abandoned or removed, and openings are closed up for the next trade to patch and paint. Permit and inspection requirements vary, so ask the Happy Valley building department about your project.

A partial repipe replaces only the failing section, such as the lines serving one bathroom or the crawlspace runs, and costs less. It makes sense when the rest of the system is sound. We tell you which approach fits when we assess the lines.

What repiping looks like in Happy Valley

Much of the city was built after 2000, and most homes have PEX or CPVC rather than old galvanized steel. A full repipe is less common here than in older nearby areas, and when it happens it is usually because of a specific failure, such as a defective fitting run, a manifold problem or repeated leaks at crawlspace runs.

In older areas around Milwaukie and Oregon City, galvanized and copper lines reaching the end of their lives are more common.

Local water from Sunrise Water Authority is soft, which is gentle on plastic and generally mild on copper, though low-mineral water can be more aggressive toward copper over decades. Clackamas River Water has noted that aging galvanized pipes can release rust particles into household water, which is a reason to replace them rather than keep repairing.

Pre-1985 homes may also have lead solder on copper joints, which is worth discussing during an assessment.

Planning around disruption

Repipes involve opening walls. The plumber chooses routes through closets, attics and crawlspaces where possible to limit damage. Expect some patching afterward. If you plan to remodel soon, coordinating the repipe with the remodel saves money, since walls are already open. We will walk you through the sequence so you know what to expect each day.

Price and scheduling

You receive a clear price before work starts. Cost depends on home size, number of fixtures, pipe material, access and whether it is a whole-house or partial job. Same-day assessments are available when the schedule allows, and the repipe itself is scheduled around your household. For smaller jobs, see our plumbing installation page.

Code, permits and safe materials

Repiping is installed to the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code (OPSC), based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and normally needs a permit and inspection. Pressure testing before walls close is the standard check. Permit rules vary, so confirm with the Happy Valley building department. Homes built before 1985 may have lead solder, which the water providers' own reports mention.
