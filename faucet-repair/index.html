A faucet that will not shut off, a spray that leaks under the sink or a handle that has snapped can flood a cabinet before you notice. If water is escaping, close the small shutoff valves under the sink by turning them clockwise. If there are no shutoffs, close the main water valve. Then call, and we will schedule a plumber to repair or replace the faucet.

Not every faucet is worth saving. Some can be restored with a new cartridge, washer or O-ring in a few minutes. Others have worn bodies or discontinued parts, and replacing them costs less than chasing repairs. The plumber checks the model and age, then tells you which route saves money.

Where faucets leak and why

Where the water appears points to the cause. A drip from the spout means a worn cartridge, ceramic disc or washer. Water around the handle means a worn O-ring or seal. A leak at the base means a failed seal or a loose mounting. Water beneath the sink might be a supply line, a shutoff valve or the faucet body itself. Pinpointing it first avoids replacing the wrong part.

Quick checks before the visit

  • Close the shutoff valves under the sink if water is escaping.
  • Dry the area and see whether the drip returns, and from where.
  • Note the brand name on the handle or spout.
  • Take a photo of the faucet and under-sink connections.

Cartridge faucets and brand specifics

Most modern faucets use a replaceable cartridge, and matching the right one matters. Brands differ in cartridge design, and some require specific tools. Our plumber identifies the make and model and installs a matched part. If a brand's parts are hard to find, the plumber tells you before work starts, and may suggest a replacement faucet instead.

Low pressure and slow flow at one faucet

If only one faucet has low flow, the problem is local, often a clogged aerator or debris in the cartridge. If every faucet is weak, the issue is the supply, a partly closed valve or a failing pressure regulator, and a plumber should check the line. Persistent drops in flow can also point to a hidden leak, which our leak detection service can trace.

Faucet replacement

When a faucet is beyond repair, we remove it and install a new one, replacing the supply lines and checking shutoff valves. Old shutoffs that will not close or that weep are replaced at the same time so the next repair is simple. If you want a kitchen faucet with a pull-down sprayer or a bathroom set with a new drain, mention it when you call.

What matters for Happy Valley faucets

Happy Valley's water is soft, so mineral crust on aerators is less common than in hard-water areas, and faucets last longer between repairs. Many local homes have faucets that are 10 to 20 years old, which is the age where cartridges and seals wear out.

Water pressure can be higher on lower streets near Mount Scott's slopes, and high pressure stresses faucet parts, so a pressure check is part of a thorough visit.

If you also have a slow kitchen drain or a garbage disposal issue, we can handle them in the same visit.

Pricing

A cartridge or seal repair costs much less than a full faucet replacement. You hear the price before work starts, including parts and labor. If you supply your own faucet, tell us when you book and we will install it.

Code and parts notes

Faucets and supply stops sold for drinking water must meet lead-free requirements under the federal Safe Drinking Water Act, and the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code (OPSC), based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), points to listed fixtures. Like-for-like repairs usually need no permit, but permit rules vary, so ask the Happy Valley building department.
