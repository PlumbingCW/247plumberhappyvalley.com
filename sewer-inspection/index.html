Sewer Inspection in Happy Valley, OR

A camera shows what a guess cannot. We inspect sewer laterals in Happy Valley for buyers, homeowners with repeat backups and anyone planning a repair, and give you recorded footage.

A sewer inspection feeds a waterproof camera on a flexible cable through the line, usually from a cleanout, and records what it sees. The footage shows pipe material, joints, cracks, offsets, sags, roots and debris. The plumber also locates problem spots at the surface so a repair, if needed, can be targeted. This page is for planned inspections.

If sewage is coming up into the house now, start with sewer line cleaning.

Inspections answer three common questions: why does this drain keep clogging, is the line safe to buy, and what exactly needs repair before anyone digs. All three save money. A camera replaces guesswork with evidence, and it prevents both unnecessary excavation and a surprise failure after closing.

Who benefits from an inspection

Home buyers use inspections because a sewer lateral is out of sight and expensive to fix. A general home inspection rarely covers it. Owners with repeat clogs use them to learn whether the cause is buildup or damage. Owners planning a remodel or a new fixture want to know the line can handle the load.

And anyone who has just had a repair can confirm the work with a follow-up pass.

What the footage can show

  • Pipe material such as PVC, ABS, cast iron or clay.
  • Cracks, broken sections and misaligned joints.
  • Sags where water pools instead of flowing.
  • Root intrusion and where it enters the line.
  • Grease, scale or debris buildup along the walls.

What happens during the visit

The plumber finds a cleanout or another access point, runs the camera through the line, and watches the monitor while recording. A locator tracks the camera head so the depth and position of any problem can be marked on the ground. Afterward, you get an explanation of what was found and whether it needs repair, cleaning or nothing at all.

Recorded footage is kept so you can see it and share it.

If the line is full of buildup, the plumber may recommend hydro jetting first so the camera can see the pipe wall. That is common in kitchen lines and does not mean there is damage, only that the footage will be clearer after cleaning.

Why it matters locally

Happy Valley's growth means many laterals are modern PVC, so inspections often find construction-era issues such as poor joint seating, debris left in the line or a section that settled where the trench was not well compacted.

In older nearby areas such as Milwaukie and Oregon City, clay and cast iron laterals bring root and age problems that a camera shows quickly. Water Environment Services manages the public sewer system in this region, so the footage also helps clarify where your responsibility ends.

If you are buying a home, a camera inspection during the due diligence period is cheap compared with a surprise sewer line repair bill after closing.

Pricing and scheduling

The inspection is priced before it starts and depends on access and line length. Planned inspections are scheduled quickly, and same-day appointments are possible when the schedule allows. If a cleanout is missing, the plumber explains how to gain access and what installing one involves.

Standards and who owns which pipe

The Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code (OPSC), based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), covers private building drains and sewers. The public main is handled by Water Environment Services, so ask them where the public line begins before you assume who pays for a repair. Keep the video file as a record.
