Your water service line is the pipe that carries water from the utility's meter to your house. In Happy Valley, Sunrise Water Authority is responsible up to the meter, and the line from the meter to the home belongs to you.

When it leaks or breaks, you may see a wet spot in the lawn, hear water running, notice a spinning meter with all fixtures off or lose pressure. This page is for failures in that buried line. If the pipe is inside the house, see burst pipe repair.

Leaks in service lines waste water and can undermine soil, driveways and foundations. If you see water pooling in the yard or the meter runs with everything off, close your house main valve and call. Closing the valve at the house stops the flow into the home, but not the leak outside, so we plan the visit quickly.

Confirming a service line leak

The first step is the meter test. Turn off all water in the house, then watch the leak indicator on the meter. If it moves, water is leaking between the meter and the house, or in the home. Closing the house main and watching the meter separates the two.

If the meter still moves with the main closed, the leak is in the buried line. Our leak detection tools then pinpoint the spot.

What to do now

  • Locate and close the main valve at the house, often in a garage or crawlspace.
  • Check the meter dial with all fixtures off and note whether it moves.
  • Mark the wet spot in the yard and take photos.
  • Do not dig near buried utilities. Call before you dig.

Repair or replace

A small leak at a joint or a short damaged section can be repaired by exposing the pipe, cutting out the damage and installing new pipe and fittings. When the line is old, corroded, made of a failing material or has failed more than once, replacement is the better value. New lines are typically installed in modern pipe sized for the home's needs.

Trenchless methods, such as pipe bursting, pull a new line through the old path with limited digging. They suit some lines and some soils, and the plumber explains whether they fit your property. A traditional trench is simpler and sometimes cheaper but disturbs landscaping.

What matters in Happy Valley

Many homes in Happy Valley were built in the last two decades, and their service lines are generally modern pipe. Failures here often come from damage during construction, poor bedding, a fitting issue or ground movement rather than age. On sloped lots near Mount Scott, line pressure can be higher, which stresses weak spots.

In older nearby areas, galvanized or copper service lines may be near the end of their life.

Digging requires locating buried utilities first, and Oregon law requires you to call the utility notification center before you dig. The plumber handles this for the work we do. Work involving the meter or utility-owned parts is for the water provider, and we explain where the boundary is on your property.

Permits and pricing

Service line work can involve permits and inspections, and rules vary by project. Ask the Happy Valley building department, and we will describe the work for your call. You receive a clear price before work begins, which depends on line length, depth, soil, surface restoration and whether trenchless methods apply.

After repair, we pressure test the line and confirm the meter is quiet before backfilling. If you are also thinking about whole-house plumbing upgrades, our repiping page covers the lines inside the home.

Code, permits and locating utilities

Water service lines are installed to the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code (OPSC), based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Your water provider owns the meter and the line up to it, so confirm who is responsible before digging. Call 811 before excavation, and ask the Happy Valley building department about permits.
