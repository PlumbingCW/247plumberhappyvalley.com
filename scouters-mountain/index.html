Why a hillside changes some plumbing questions

Happy Valley sits on the southeast flank of Mount Scott, and elevation varies across town. That affects water pressure at your tap. Pressure is set on the supply side, so two homes on the same hill can differ. A cheap gauge on an outdoor hose bib shows your static pressure in a minute.

Code generally calls for a pressure regulator when supply pressure is above about 80 psi. High pressure is hard on water heaters, toilet fill valves and faucet cartridges, and it can show up as banging pipes or a relief valve that drips. If your gauge reads high, mention it when you call. Our water heater repair page covers the relief valve.

Slopes and drainage

On a slope, surface water moves toward lower yards, and drains inside the house depend on gravity to the sewer. Slow drains in a lower-level bathroom are worth reporting early. If a drain backs up in the lowest fixture of a house, stop using water above it and call, since the blockage may be in the main line.

Hose bibs, irrigation and shut-offs

Outdoor spigots on a hillside lot are often the longest runs from the main. In a cold snap, disconnect hoses and close the interior shut-off to the bib if your house has one. Know where your main shut-off is. The main is often in the garage or crawlspace, but confirm yours so you are not searching when water is running.

Newer homes, older homes

If your home is under a builder warranty, read the terms before a repair. Some warranties want the builder contacted first. For older homes, ask about the age of the water heater and look for corrosion at fittings. Neither is a sign of a problem on its own, only a prompt to have it checked. Our leak detection page explains how hidden leaks are found without opening walls.

Before you call

  • Close the main shut-off valve if water is running.
  • Read your water pressure from a hose bib gauge if you have one.
  • Note which fixtures are affected and when it started.
  • Photograph the water heater label and any drips.
  • Check your builder warranty paperwork if the home is new.

Code and permits

Oregon plumbing follows the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Permit rules vary, so ask the Happy Valley building department before replacing a water heater or changing pipe layout. Our Happy Valley home page lists all our services.
