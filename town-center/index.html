Plumber at the Happy Valley Town Center, OR

The Happy Valley Town Center is a mixed-use area of shops, offices and apartments along Sunnyside Road. Plumbing here depends on whether you rent, own or run a business.

The Happy Valley Town Center is not a typical subdivision. It is a mixed-use district around SE Sunnyside Road with retail, offices, City Hall at 16000 SE Misty Dr and apartment communities. The city planned a Town Center of about 225 acres, and apartment communities such as Town Center Heights, Sunridge Terrace and Town Center Courtyards are listed in the area.

Because it mixes homes and businesses, this page is written for three readers: apartment residents, small property owners and people who run a shop or office. Each has a different first step.

Where it sits

Along SE Sunnyside Rd in central Happy Valley. A 2011 city report described a planned Town Center of about 225 acres.

ZIP code

The area uses 97086, the Happy Valley ZIP code.

Water and sewer

Happy Valley is mostly served by Sunrise Water Authority, and Water Environment Services handles sewer. Your lease or bill shows your provider.

If you rent an apartment here

Call your property manager first. In a rental the landlord usually controls repairs, and many leases name the plumber or require approval. What you can do right away is protect the unit. Close the shut-off valve under a leaking sink or toilet, and move belongings away from water. Take photos with the date, and report in writing so there is a record.

Do not try to open the building main or shut off water to other units. If water is coming from a ceiling, tell the manager at once and say so when you call anyone.

If you own a unit or a small property

Owners of condos and small buildings need to know where their responsibility ends. Association documents usually say whether pipes inside the wall belong to you or to the association. A leak between floors can involve several owners. Ask the association before work starts, and keep the plumber's written findings. Our leak detection page explains how a source is traced without opening more than needed.

If you run a shop, office or restaurant

Commercial plumbing here follows the same Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, but extra rules can apply. Grease interceptors for food service and backflow devices on certain lines are regulated by the local sewer and water agencies, and requirements vary. Confirm them with Water Environment Services and the Happy Valley building department before work. Our commercial plumbing page describes the work we do for businesses.

Common business calls include slow floor drains, toilets that run in busy restrooms, water heaters that cannot keep up and leaks near equipment. A drain cleaning visit clears most slow drains, and repeated clogs justify a camera look.

Shared lines and who pays

In multifamily buildings a clog may be in a branch that serves one unit or in a stack that serves several. If one unit backs up and the neighbors are fine, the problem is usually local. If several units are affected, it points to the shared line, which is the building owner's responsibility.

Before you call

  • Say whether you rent, own or manage the property.
  • Name your building and unit, and give the street address.
  • Close the local shut-off if water is running.
  • Take dated photos and note when it started.
  • Notify your landlord, association or manager at the same time.

Code and permits

Permit rules vary by project and by whether the work is residential or commercial. Ask the Happy Valley building department. Our Happy Valley home page lists all services.
