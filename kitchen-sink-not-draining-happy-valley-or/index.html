Kitchen Sink Not Draining but Not Clogged? A Happy Valley Guide

Sometimes a kitchen sink will not drain and there is no obvious clog. The causes are usually hidden in the vent, the trap arm or the line beyond the disposal.

By the Happy Valley 24/7 Plumbing teamPublished October 16, 2026Updated November 13, 2026

A kitchen sink that drains slowly or not at all, with no solid object stuck in the drain, still has a cause. The usual causes are a grease-lined pipe that has narrowed over time, a partial blockage past the garbage disposal or a blocked vent that stops air from entering the line.

A main line problem can also show up first at the kitchen, because it is the lowest or farthest fixture.

Work through the likely causes in order of how easy they are to check. Most take a few minutes, and the first few cost nothing. If you reach the end of the list without a fix, the problem is deeper than a household tool can reach, and a cable or camera is the next step.

Start with the simple checks

Remove the strainer basket and look for debris. Run hot water and see whether the sink drains faster in one basin than the other. If one basin drains and the other does not, the blockage is in the branch between them, often at the tailpiece or trap.

Put a bucket under the trap, unscrew the slip nuts and clean out the trap. A gelatinous mass of grease and food particles is the classic find.

Check the disposal and dishwasher connection

If you have a garbage disposal, the drain beyond it can clog even when the disposal runs fine. A dishwasher drain hose that ties into the disposal inlet can also back up if the knockout plug was never removed, or if food debris blocks the connection. Run the dishwasher while watching the sink.

If water rises in the sink basin, the shared line is restricted. Our garbage disposal repair page covers the unit itself.

What not to do

  • Do not pour chemical drain cleaner into a sink with standing water.
  • Do not run the disposal while the sink is full of water and the drain is blocked.
  • Do not use a long metal snake without knowing what is in the line.
  • Do not keep running the dishwasher into a backed-up sink.

When the vent is the problem

A drain needs air behind the water to flow. The vent pipe, which usually runs up through the roof, supplies that air. If a vent clogs with debris, a nest or ice, the drain gurgles and drains slowly, even though the pipe is clear.

Signs include gurgling from other fixtures, a sucking sound as water goes down and slow drainage after heavy use. A plumber can check the vent with a camera or by running water on the roof side.

Grease-lined pipes

Kitchen drains collect fat, oil and food film. Over years the buildup narrows the pipe until even a small clog stops it. A cable can open a channel through it, but the film remains, and the problem returns in weeks. Hydro jetting scours the pipe walls and gives a lasting result. A camera shows how much buildup there is and whether the pipe is damaged.

Prevention that works

Wipe greasy pans before washing, scrape plates into the trash and use a strainer. Run hot water for 15 to 30 seconds after the dishes. Do not pour grease or oil down the drain. A monthly rinse with very hot water, not boiling water on plastic pipe, helps keep film from building. These habits matter more than any product.

Happy Valley note: newer pipe, different problems

Much of Happy Valley was built after 2000, with roughly 1,500 housing units in 2000 and over 8,000 by 2020, so most local kitchen drains are PVC rather than old metal pipe. Those smooth pipes clear well, which makes a kitchen blockage in a newer home more likely to be grease, a vent issue or a construction leftover than corrosion.

If the kitchen sink backs up along with other fixtures, the lateral to the public sewer may be involved. Water Environment Services, the Clackamas County agency that manages sewer and stormwater, handles the public main, and the lateral from your house to it is generally yours.

When to call a plumber

Call when a cable or plunger does not fix it, when more than one fixture is slow, when you smell sewer gas or when the problem returns. If you are in Oregon City or Milwaukie, where older cast iron and clay laterals are common, a recurring slow drain deserves a camera inspection soon, since the cause may be in the buried line.

A five-minute routine for the next slow drain

When a kitchen sink slows down, a short routine can save a service call. Clear the strainer, run hot water for a full minute and watch the rate. Clean the trap if you can reach it, put a bucket under it first. Run the dishwasher briefly to see whether the sink rises.

If the drain is quicker afterward, the problem was grease film or a soft clog, and the habits above will keep it from returning.

If the routine does not help, stop. Heavy plunging and repeated chemicals make a stubborn clog harder for the plumber to clear and can push waste further into the line. Write down which fixtures are affected and whether the sink gurgles, and call with that information.

Code and safety reference

Sink drains and vents follow the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code (OPSC), based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Skip mixed chemical cleaners, and call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 after any splash on skin or eyes.
