If you are reading this with water on the floor, close the main valve and call. Damascus lies just east of Happy Valley, and our line is open every hour. Tell us your address, what is leaking or backing up and whether the water is off, and we will take it from there.

Damascus has a particular local history, and it shapes who you call about what. The city incorporated in 2004, and after voters chose to disincorporate in 2016, the Oregon Supreme Court upheld that result on September 3, 2020. Today Damascus is unincorporated Clackamas County, which means county and special district services rather than a city hall handle most local questions, including permits.

Start with what kind of property you have

Damascus properties range from small subdivisions to acreage lots, and that changes the plumbing questions. A home on a larger lot may have a long water service line from the road, which is a candidate for water line repair when the lawn turns soggy.

A property that is not on a public sewer may be on a septic system, and the right fix for a backup is different. Tell us which you have when you call.

We do not guess about septic systems. If your property uses one, the plumber works on the house side, such as fixtures, traps and drain lines, and will say clearly when a problem belongs to a septic professional instead.

Water from Sunrise Water Authority

Sunrise Water Authority serves Damascus along with Happy Valley and parts of unincorporated Clackamas County. Its supply is generally soft, about 1.2 grains per gallon, and comes mainly from the Clackamas River with local wells added at peak demand. Sunrise is responsible up to the meter. The line from the meter to your house is yours, and that line is where we are called most.

A meter that spins with everything off, a wet patch between the road and the house or a sudden pressure drop usually mean the buried line. A leak detection visit confirms it, and the plumber lays out repair or replacement choices before any digging.

Rural and acreage plumbing issues

Longer pipe runs, outbuildings and outdoor spigots all create more places for water to escape. Hose bibs that stay connected through cold snaps can split. A detached shop with a water line can lose pressure when a fitting fails underground. Because pipes run farther, pressure at the far end can be lower, and a pressure test shows whether the regulator or the line itself is the cause.

Getting your property ready for the plumber

Before We Arrive

  • Close the main valve at the house, and the isolation valve at any outbuilding.
  • Check the meter indicator, and note whether it moves with all water off.
  • Switch off power to a leaking water heater and keep everyone out of wet areas.
  • Tell us whether the property is on public sewer or a septic system.
  • Share gate codes, long driveway details or dog notes in advance.
  • Take photos of any wet ground or damaged materials for your insurance file.

What we see most in Damascus homes

Many newer Damascus homes are builder-grade with early-2000s water heaters nearing the end of their service life, so water heater replacement is a common call. The right approach depends on what the line is made of and where it runs.
