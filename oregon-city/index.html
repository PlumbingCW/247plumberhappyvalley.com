Oregon City has more history per block than almost anywhere in Clackamas County. It was the first incorporated city west of the Rocky Mountains, in 1844, and the end of the Oregon Trail. That history is part of why plumbing calls here often turn on the age of the house, and why the first question we ask is when it was built.

Old neighborhoods, old pipes

Canemah and the McLoughlin area carry some of the oldest housing in the county, and the city recognizes historic districts there. Older homes on these streets can have galvanized supply lines, cast iron drains and cellar plumbing that was updated piece by piece. Historic status can add rules about exterior changes, so check with the city before altering anything visible from the street.

When one of these houses has a failure, the repair is rarely isolated. A leak in galvanized pipe suggests more corrosion nearby. A drain that backs up often has a long history of roots and patches. We assess before we quote, and we recommend a staged plan when that costs less than repairing one break at a time. Our repiping and sewer line repair pages explain both.

Sewer lines on hills

The city runs a sanitary sewer rehabilitation program, including projects in the McLoughlin basin, aimed at reducing groundwater and stormwater that leak into old sewer pipes. That is useful background if your area has a history of backups. Inflow into the public system is a city concern, and your lateral is a private one.

A camera run through your own line shows whether roots, offsets or old clay joints are the problem.

Steep streets also change drainage. A house below the street may have a lateral with limited fall to the main, and a backup there floods the lowest level quickly. Where that applies, we discuss a backwater prevention option and a regular cleaning plan. For single-fixture trouble, drain cleaning is the place to start.

Water from the river

Many Oregon City customers receive water drawn from the Clackamas River through the South Fork Water Board. The city notes that as areas are annexed, they move out of Clackamas River Water service, so your provider can depend on your address. Confirm yours before you call a utility about a meter question.

Quick steps that limit damage

Before We Arrive

  • Shut off the main water valve, often in a basement, a cellar or a crawlspace.
  • Turn off the water heater power or gas, and stay out of water near outlets.
  • Clear a path down steep or narrow basement stairs for the plumber.
  • Move valuables off basement floors where water pools first.
  • Tell us the neighborhood, the approximate age of the house and any prior plumbing work.
  • Photograph damage and note times for your insurance claim.

What brings Oregon City callers to us

Hot water failures in older basements lead to water heater repair or replacement with proper bracing and a drain pan. Cracked or leaking drain lines lead to a camera visit. Hidden moisture in a crawlspace leads to leak detection, which finds the source before anyone opens walls.

Codes and agencies in Oregon City

Oregon City plumbing follows the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code (OPSC), based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Historic district projects in Canemah or McLoughlin may carry extra review, so ask the city before altering visible exteriors or piping.
