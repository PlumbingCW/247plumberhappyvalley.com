Rock Creek is a name Happy Valley residents actually use. The city adopted a Rock Creek Comprehensive Plan on June 5, 2001 for land in this part of town, so the name is more than a real estate label. This page is for people who live or work here and want plain answers before they call about a drain, a water heater or a leak.

The plan for Rock Creek allows a mix of housing. Its mixed use residential land averages about 12 units per acre, and attached housing is allowed near commercial land. In practice you will find houses, townhomes and apartment communities such as Rock Creek Ridge. That mix matters for plumbing, because the first question is often who is responsible for the pipe.

Where it sits

On the south side of Happy Valley in Clackamas County. The edges are informal, so we go by your street address and ZIP code.

ZIP codes

Addresses here use 97015 or 97086, and some carry a Clackamas mailing name even though they are in Happy Valley.

Water and sewer

Sunrise Water Authority or Clackamas River Water supplies the water depending on the street. Water Environment Services handles sewer and stormwater. Check your bill.

Who fixes what in a mixed housing area

Responsibility changes with the kind of home you have. Sort that out first and you save a call and sometimes a bill.

  • Owner of a detached house: the plumbing from the meter to every fixture is yours, and you choose the plumber.
  • Renter: report leaks, backups and no hot water to your landlord or property manager first.
  • Townhome owner: check your association rules, because some shared lines and exterior work follow community rules.
  • Apartment resident: shut-off valves under sinks and toilets are yours to use, but the building main is not.

If you are not sure where your responsibility ends, call and describe the problem. The person who answers will tell you what to check.

Homes of different ages need different questions

Listing sites describe many Rock Creek homes as built between the late 1980s and the early 2000s, with newer homes mixed in. Treat that as a rough guide, not a fact about your house. We do not assume what your pipes are made of. The plumber identifies the material on site and explains what it means for repair options.

One simple check helps in any home. Look at the label on your water heater for the manufacture date. If it dates from the original construction, ask about its age when you call, because a tank that has run for many years is worth planning around. Our water heater repair page explains how repair and replacement are compared.

The creek corridor and stormwater

A city report from 2011 notes the Rock Creek corridor for stormwater integration and a planned Rock Creek Trail. For homeowners the practical point is that roof drains, yard drains and sump discharge are not the same as the sanitary sewer. Do not connect a sump pump discharge to a sewer cleanout or a drain inside the house. Ask Water Environment Services where it is allowed to go.

Common calls from this area

Most calls from Rock Creek homes are for slow or clogged drains, water heaters that stop heating, running toilets and leaks that show up as stains or a high water bill. If one fixture drains slowly, try a plunger first. If several fixtures back up together, stop using water and call, because that points to the main line. Our toilet repair and leak detection pages cover those two cases.

Before you call

  • Note your street address and the nearest cross street.
  • Find your main water shut-off valve and close it if water is running.
  • Take photos of any stains, wet floors or error codes.
  • Check whether the problem affects one fixture or the whole house.
  • If you rent, tell your landlord or property manager at the same time.

Code and permits

Plumbing in Oregon follows the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Permit rules vary by project, so ask the Happy Valley building department whether yours needs one.
