Why Clackamas homes have mixed plumbing

Clackamas is a long-settled area that grew in waves. Homes near the older commercial corridors can date back decades, while subdivisions along the newer roads are recent. That mix means one street can have galvanized or copper supply lines and the next can have PEX. A plumber who knows to ask the age of the house is already halfway to the answer, so expect that question when you call.

Older homes tend to bring cast iron or clay drain laterals, roots and corrosion. Newer ones bring PVC and fittings that fail in different ways. The right fix depends on which one you have, and that is why we look before we quote.

Water here is soft, and that changes what we look for

Clackamas River Water draws from the Clackamas River and reports average hardness of about 15 parts per million in winter and about 29 in summer, which is soft. Soft water means heating elements and tankless heat exchangers collect little scale, so a water heater that fails here is more often worn out than scaled up. It also means mineral crust on faucets is rare.

The utility notes that white flakes in hot water can come from a disintegrating dip tube in the heater, and rust-colored water can come from aging galvanized pipes. Both are worth acting on. A flake problem points to a repair or replacement of the heater, and a rust problem points to the pipes, which we assess for repiping when repairs keep repeating.

Checking your own meter first

Clackamas River Water suggests a simple leak check. Turn off everything that uses water, find the meter and watch the small triangular or wheel indicator for about 20 minutes. If it moves, water is going somewhere. That result tells us whether to look inside the house or at the buried line, and it is useful information to have ready when you call.

Steps to take while help is on the way

Before We Arrive

  • Shut off the main valve, usually in the garage, a crawlspace or a utility room.
  • Turn off power to a leaking water heater at its breaker, or the gas control to pilot.
  • Move rugs, boxes and electronics away from wet floors, and keep children out of the area.
  • Photograph the damage and note when the problem started for your insurance claim.
  • Leave drains alone if they are backing up, and do not use chemical cleaners.
  • Tell us the street address, cross street and whether there is a gate or a dog.

Sewer and who handles what

Water Environment Services manages the public sewer and stormwater system in this part of Clackamas County. The public main sits in the street, and the pipe from your house to that main is generally yours. If only your home is backing up, the problem is probably on your lateral.

If neighbors are affected too, the public main may be involved, and the agency is the right call after we confirm the blockage with a camera.

When a main line backs up, sewer line cleaning clears the immediate problem, and a camera shows why it happened. Roots are common in older lots, and repeat blockages point to a section that needs sewer line repair.
