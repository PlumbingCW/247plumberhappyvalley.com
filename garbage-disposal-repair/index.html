A disposal that hums but does not spin is jammed. A disposal that does nothing may have tripped its reset button or lost power. A disposal that leaks has a failed seal, a loose connection or a cracked housing. Each has a different fix, and some can be handled in minutes.

Please keep your hands out of the unit at all times, and switch off the power at the breaker before any attempt to clear a jam.

The reset button on the bottom of the disposal pops out when the motor overheats or jams. Wait a few minutes and press it. If it trips again right away, there is either a jam or a motor problem. A plumber can free a jam with the correct wrench and tell you whether the motor is worth saving.

Common disposal problems

Jams from bones, fruit pits, utensils or fibrous vegetables are the most frequent, and a plumber clears them by turning the motor by hand from the bottom. Leaks come from the sink flange, the drain connection or the housing. Slow draining and gurgling usually mean a clog beyond the disposal, which becomes a drain cleaning job. Bad odors come from buildup, not a defect.

Safety first

  • Turn off power to the disposal at the breaker before touching anything.
  • Never put your hand into the grinding chamber, even if the power seems off.
  • Do not use chemical drain cleaners in a disposal.
  • Run cold water, not hot, when grinding to push grease through.

Repair versus replacement

A disposal in decent shape with a simple jam is worth repairing. A unit that leaks from the body, has a seized motor or is very old is cheaper to replace. Typical disposals last around a decade depending on use. The plumber gives you the numbers and lets you choose.

When replacing, we match mounting hardware, check the dishwasher connection and test for leaks. If you want a larger motor for heavy use, mention it. We can also help if your home has a double sink drain that was never installed correctly, which causes frequent backups.

Disposal care that prevents calls

Run cold water while grinding and for 15 seconds after. Avoid grease, coffee grounds in bulk, bones, pasta and rice in large amounts, and fibrous peels. Cut food waste into small pieces and feed it gradually. Never pour hot grease down any drain. If your kitchen drain is slow even with the disposal off, the line may need hydro jetting.

Disposals in Happy Valley kitchens

Many kitchens in Happy Valley were built in the last 20 years and came with builder-grade disposals sized for light use. Those units often start leaking or seizing around the 8 to 12 year mark. Soft local water means less mineral buildup inside the housing, but grease and food film still cause odors and clogs.

A kitchen with a disposal also needs a properly vented and trapped drain, which we check as part of the visit.

If your faucet is also dripping under the sink, a faucet repair can be combined into the same appointment.

Pricing

A jam clearing costs far less than a replacement. You hear the price before work begins, with parts and labor listed. If the plumber finds that the drain beyond the disposal is blocked, they tell you before moving on to that work.

Safety first with disposals

Never put a hand in a disposal, even when it is off. Switch it off at the wall or breaker, and use the hex socket under the unit or a wooden spoon handle from above to free a jam. Disposal hookups follow the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code (OPSC), based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), including the trap and air gap or high loop on the dishwasher line.
