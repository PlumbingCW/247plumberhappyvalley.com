High Water Bill With No Visible Leak in Happy Valley, OR

A bill that doubles without explanation usually has a cause you can find in ten minutes with the water meter. Here is the step-by-step way to check.

By the Happy Valley 24/7 Plumbing teamPublished September 18, 2026Updated November 13, 2026

A water bill that jumps with no change in how you live is almost always one of three things: a running toilet, a leak you cannot see, or irrigation that is using more than you think. You can sort them out yourself in about ten minutes using the meter at the street.

If the meter keeps moving while every tap is off, water is escaping somewhere, and the next step is finding where.

Most utilities bill by the volume that passes the meter, so a leak you cannot see still shows up in numbers. A pinhole leak at 10 gallons an hour is 240 gallons a day, which is enough to double a modest household bill without any visible puddle.

The ten-minute meter test

First, turn off every tap, appliance and irrigation zone, and make sure no one flushes a toilet. Open the meter box and look for the small leak indicator, usually a triangle or a tiny wheel that turns when any water moves. Watch it for a few minutes.

Clackamas River Water suggests watching the triangle dial for about 20 minutes to be sure. If it turns, water is leaking. If it stays still, the leak is not continuous, and the cause is more likely a toilet or a fixture that runs on and off.

Do this in order

  • Stop all water use, including the ice maker and any irrigation.
  • Record the meter reading and the time.
  • Wait 30 minutes with nothing running, then read it again.
  • If the number changed, close the main valve at the house and check again.

Narrowing it down with the main valve

Closing the house main valve splits the problem in two. If the meter stops after you close it, the leak is inside the house or at a fixture. If the meter keeps turning with the main closed, the leak is on the buried line between the meter and the house. That single test tells a plumber where to look and often saves hours of searching.

The usual suspects inside the house

A toilet is the most common culprit. A worn flapper lets water seep into the bowl all day with no sound. Drop a few drops of food coloring in the tank, wait 15 minutes without flushing, and see if color shows up in the bowl. Dripping faucets and a leaking water heater relief valve add up as well.

An outdoor hose bib that leaks inside the wall can waste water for months.

When the leak is outside

A buried service line can leak for a long time before it surfaces. Look for a soggy patch, unusually green grass in one strip, or a depression near the line. Because the water goes into the soil, you may never see a puddle. If you suspect the buried line, see our water line repair page for what the repair involves.

How we find hidden leaks without demolition

Acoustic listening finds the sound of pressurized water escaping from a pipe. Thermal imaging shows temperature differences in walls and floors. Pressure testing isolates sections of piping to show which one is losing pressure. Used together, they narrow the problem to a small area. Our leak detection page explains the process and pricing, and the plumber tells you the price before the search begins.

Happy Valley note: who owns which pipe

Sunrise Water Authority, which serves Happy Valley, Carver and Damascus, is responsible for the water system up to the outlet side of the meter. Everything after the meter, including the line to your house, is the homeowner's.

Sunrise also notes that its customer main shutoff is typically in the garage or crawlspace and that its emergency line is answered 24 hours a day for problems on its side. If you live in Clackamas, parts of the area are served by Clackamas River Water, which publishes the same leak-check guidance for its meters.

What to do about the bill

If you find a leak, repair it, then ask your water provider whether it offers any adjustment for a documented leak. Policies vary and none is certain, so ask before assuming. Keep your repair invoice and the plumber's written description of what was found.

If your bill is high but no leak turns up, compare the billing periods against your household's usage, such as visitors, a new appliance, filling a pool or heavier irrigation.

Residents in Clackamas and Damascus sometimes have longer service lines from the meter to the house, which gives a buried leak more room to hide. That is worth mentioning when you call.

Seasonal and household causes worth ruling out

Not every spike is a leak. Irrigation season can double usage within a month, especially if a timer runs on a schedule set years ago. Guests, a new baby, a filled pool or a new appliance with a big water draw all change the numbers.

Compare the bill against the same months last year, and look at the meter reading dates, because a longer billing period alone can make a bill look higher.

If the meter test shows no movement and your habits have not changed, ask your provider for a usage history. Daily or monthly readings show whether the rise was steady, which suggests a continuous leak, or sudden, which suggests an event such as a stuck valve or a broken sprinkler line.

Reference for your meter check

Your water provider, such as Clackamas River Water or Sunrise Water Authority, publishes how to read the meter and test for a leak. Their guidance describes the same dial check described here. Any repair afterward follows the Oregon Plumbing Specialty Code (OPSC), based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).
